# Story: Bad Paper, a Dark Forces prequel

The story for Rogue Contract. The direction is chosen (below), and the
details are still a draft to refine.

## Decided so far (Mark, 2026-10-13)

- **Bad Paper**, the bounty-hunter idea (Idea 2 of the first draft). The
  other idea, Dead Drop, is set aside, though its Hutt palace could come
  back as a later mission.
- **A prequel to Dark Forces.** It's set a few months before Dark Forces'
  first mission (Danuta, where Kyle steals the Death Star plans), so shortly
  before *A New Hope*. Dark Forces belongs to the old "Legends" continuity,
  and *Rogue One* tells the plans' theft differently. Where the two
  disagree, we follow Dark Forces.
- **The hero is Kyle Katarn.** This replaces PLAN.md's earlier "no Kyle
  Katarn retelling".
- **Jan Ors flies Kyle in and out in the Moldy Crow,** as in Dark Forces:
  each mission starts with a drop-off and ends with a pickup.
- **The first mission opens with the Moldy Crow landing in a Mos Eisley
  docking bay.**
- **Only Mission 1 is free roam.** Kyle explores a calm Mos Eisley with the
  tracking fob. The gunfire starts when he finds his target, and from then
  on it's straight run-and-gun, for the rest of Mission 1 and every mission
  after it. Later missions have no stealth and no tracker.

## How it fits Dark Forces

**Kyle and Jan.** In Legends, Kyle leaves the Empire, with Jan Ors's help,
not long before Danuta. This story sits in that gap. Kyle is a freelance
mercenary, and Jan, a Rebel agent, brings him work and flies the Crow. The
blaster already uses Dark Forces' Bryar pistol art, which is Kyle's own
gun.

**Mohc's project.** In Dark Forces, General Rom Mohc builds the Dark
Troopers: droid soldiers in three phases, armoured in phrik, a metal mined
on Gromas 16 that blasters can't get through. The Rebellion learns they
exist only after one wipes out its base on Talay (Dark Forces' second
mission).

Our link: **the Canyon Garrison on Tatooine is an early field test of
Mohc's project.** It's a prototype from before Phase I, a crude assault
droid built before Mohc had phrik, so blasters do hurt it. This is Phase
3's planned mini-boss ("a prototype assault droid").

- **Why Tatooine:** it's far from the Core, nobody asks questions, and the
  Jundland Wastes are full of targets: Tusken camps and isolated farms.
- **What Kyle does:** destroys the prototype and gets the test records out.
  The test failed, and in Legends terms that's why Mohc needs a tougher
  armour, and so phrik.
- **The irony of a prequel:** the Alliance files Kyle's report as
  unconfirmed: one prototype, destroyed, no sign of others. Months later, on
  Talay, he sees one again.
- **Mohc:** Kyle glimpses him once, through a window, and doesn't learn his
  name. They never fight, because he's Dark Forces' final boss.
- **The ending leads straight into Dark Forces:** the Alliance's next job
  for Kyle is Danuta.

## The story

**The job.** The Bounty Hunters' Guild has posted a quiet contract on Arlen
Dace, a scout trooper who deserted and has gone to ground in Mos Eisley.
Jan's Alliance contacts say he saw something in the Jundland, and they want
him before the Empire gets him. The Alliance can't go openly, so Kyle takes
the Guild contract as cover. A licensed hunter can walk anywhere in Mos
Eisley, troopers wave hunters through, and the contract comes with a
tracking fob. The licence is in a false name, which Jan made. Kyle is a
wanted deserter himself, so it had better hold. Kyle also notes that the
Guild pays double if Dace is breathing, and that he means to collect from
both.

**What Dace knows.** His scout unit guarded the garrison's test range in the
Jundland. He watched the prototype destroy a Tusken camp. Then his unit was
ordered to clear a moisture farm so the prototype could be tested on it. He
refused, warned the family and ran. They hid him for a while, then he came
to Mos Eisley to buy passage off-world. Like Kyle, he walked away from the
Empire, and the story uses that.

**The twist.** Mohc's people posted the contract through a front. They don't
want Dace back. They want him silenced, and they want to know who he told.
The fob carries an Imperial tracer, so the moment Kyle finds Dace, the
Empire has found him too. Customs also logged the Moldy Crow when it landed,
so they know who the hunter really is. The contract has gone rogue.

## The Moldy Crow: drop-offs and pickups

Every mission starts and ends with the Crow, as Dark Forces did between
missions. Here they happen in the level itself, because the open sky and 3D
models suit it.

**The drop-off** (about 10 seconds, skipped with use or fire). Mission 1:

1. The level opens on a fixed camera on the rim of Docking Bay 87, looking
   down into it. The Crow comes down out of the sky, turning as it settles.
   The engine roar rises, dust blows across the bay floor, and its landing
   lights glow.
2. Cut to Kyle at the foot of the ramp, facing the bay door. Control
   returns, with the HUD.
   - JAN: Customs will be all over this bay in two minutes. I'm not staying
     for them.
   - KYLE: I'll call when I've got him.
3. Behind Kyle the Crow lifts off, climbs out of the bay and flies away over
   the rooftops. Turn round and you watch it go.
4. The customs officer and two troopers come in through the bay door, calm.
   "Bay 87. Your ship left without clearance, hunter." They check the
   licence and let Kyle through.

Missions 2 and 3 use the same pieces at their own spots (the ridge, the
start bowl), shorter and without the customs scene.

**The pickup.** Walk onto the marked pickup spot, and the Crow comes down
onto it. Control locks, JAN says a line, and the level ends once the Crow
has landed. The Canyon Garrison already ends this way at its pad. Mission 2
adds a wait: the spot only works once the Crow arrives, so you hold it
under fire until Jan lands.

**Not doing:** walking down the ramp out of the hold. The grid can't keep a
room inside a ship that then flies away, so Kyle starts at the foot of the
ramp.

## Mission 1's free roam

**Calm characters.** Until the shooting starts, the troopers in Mos Eisley
are calm. They stand their posts or walk patrol routes, say a line when you
bump or use them ("Move along." "Hunter."), and don't attack. Townsfolk,
Jawa traders and the cantina's customers are friendly characters, as the
engine has today.

**One switch.** The town turns hostile all at once, and it has two causes:

- **The story:** Kyle finds Dace, and a trigger flips it.
- **You:** fire a weapon or hit anyone, and the town flips early. That
  doesn't fail the mission. The fob still leads to Dace, and the hunt for
  him becomes a fight.

When it flips, every calm trooper becomes a normal monster, blast doors
close across the main street, reinforcements come out of the prefecture,
and civilians run for cover. The music switches to the combat track.

**The tracking fob.** Taken from the tracking fob in *The Mandalorian*: a
small handheld unit whose light blinks faster as you close in.

- **You hold it like a weapon.** It has its own slot, and its first-person
  sprite shows its blinking light. While you hold it, your blaster is
  holstered.
- **It tracks one signal.** It blinks and beeps faster as you get closer,
  turns from amber to green when you face the signal, and stays dark when
  there's nothing to track. Triggers set the signal: a tagged spot, an item
  or a character.
- **It lives only in Mission 1.** It starts dark, picks up the Jawas'
  transponder code and leads to Dace. Then Dace pulls the tracer out of it,
  Kyle drops it, and the slot is gone for the rest of the game.

**No stealth.** There are no restricted zones, no warnings and no
detection. The closed checkpoint is an exploration puzzle: get a pass from
an informant, or climb over the rooftops.

## Map A: Mos Eisley (Mission 1)

A loop. Kyle walks east through a calm town in daylight, finds Dace at the
far end, then fights back west through the same streets to the bay he
started in. The second half reuses the first half's geometry, with every
trooper you passed now shooting.

- **Size:** about 150 × 110 tiles (75 × 55 m). The Canyon Garrison is
  128 × 100.
- **Light:** sunlit streets at 200-224 under a new day sky with both suns,
  alleys and doorways in shade at 136-152, and dim interiors. The other
  missions are at night, so this is the only daylight map.
- **Buildings:** in Doom's style. The street's sky sits just above the
  rooftops, so a building's wall stops at its roofline and the sky shows
  above it. Rooftops you can walk on are raised floors under a higher sky.
  Round adobe shapes come from chamfers and octagons (angled walls), and
  domes are 3D models.

```
 west                                        ║                         east
  [4 Jawa junk yard]  [7 alleys]             ║  [8 prefecture]
          |                 |                ║        |
  ======= 2 main street (market) =======  [5 checkpoint] == 9 old quarter == [10 derelict bay: Dace]
          |                 |                ║
  [1 Docking Bay 87]   [3 cantina]           ║  [6 rooftops: a way over 5]
```

**Going east (calm):** the Crow drops Kyle in the bay → the cantina
(contract and fob) → the junk yard (a signal) → past the checkpoint, with
a pass or over the roofs → the old quarter → the derelict bay (Dace).

**Coming back west (loud):** the derelict bay → the old quarter → through
the checkpoint (now open) or over the roofs, with troopers on the roofs this
time → the main street's blast doors are shut, so through the cantina or
the back alleys → the bay, where the Crow lands.

maintenance pit in Bay 87's floor where the Crow landed (a shield unit),
and a rooftop cache with a better weapon.

## Map B: The Homestead (Mission 2)

The farm that hid Dace, at the edge of the Jundland, at night. The raid is
already under way when Kyle arrives. All combat.

- **Size:** about 110 × 90 tiles.
- **Light:** the night sky we have. The farm is lit by fires (flickering
  light around burning buildings) and by its doorways.

```
            [7 ridge: Tuskens]

**The ending:** picking up the recordings starts it. Heavy footsteps, then
the prototype's shape in the dark across the vaporator field. The objective
says run. Kyle calls the Crow, and JAN: "Two minutes!" Hold the pickup spot
until she lands.

**Rooms under the ground:** with two storeys per tile (PLAN.md Phase 1c,
done), the rooms round the courtyard can lie under walkable ground: their
tiles have a slab whose top is the surface. Their doorways into the
courtyard need a door in a slab tile, which isn't supported yet, so give
the doorways open arches or add that first.

## Map C: The Canyon Garrison (Mission 3, changes to the existing map)

`docs/mission3.md` describes the map, and it stays a combat mission entered
by the vent. This story adds:

- **The drop-off** in the start bowl, which is already open to the sky.
- **The general behind the barred window.** The existing window from the
  barracks corridor into the officer's office shows Mohc (a character you
  can't shoot) hurrying out of a far door as Kyle passes. He's gone before
  Kyle can get round.
- **A detention block** off the barracks corridor, behind bars (existing
  fences). Kyle opens Dace's cell, and Dace heads for the pad on his own.
  He's removed here and placed at the pad, so he needs no escort AI.
- **The vault** holds the test records, the mission's objective.
- **The prototype in the generator chamber,** as the boss. Blasters wear it
  down slowly. The overload finishes it.
- **The pickup** at the pad's marked spot, which is already the exit.
  Mohc's shuttle has gone (the placeholder shuttle model leaves the pad),
  and the Crow comes down for Kyle and Dace.

## Engine work this needs

The build list, in order and with the code each piece touches, is in
PLAN.md under Phase 2 ("For the story"). In short: most of this builds on
code we already have: the friendly NPCs
(`things/npc.js`: wandering, lines when bumped), monster wake-up
(`things/monster.js`), model decorations (which already update every tick,
for `spin`), the single `world.camera()` the renderer draws from, and
triggers and actions.

| Feature | What it does | Built from | Size | Used in |
|---------|--------------|------------|------|---------|
| **Calm troopers and the town's switch** | A placed monster can start `calm`: it stands its post or wanders, says lines when bumped or used, and doesn't attack. A `hostile` action flips every calm monster on the level to a normal monster at once. Firing a weapon or hitting anyone while calm monsters are on the level runs it too. An `on: 'hostile'` trigger closes doors, spawns the reinforcements and changes the music. | NPC and monster code | Small to medium | M1 |
| **Patrol routes** | `patrol: 'street'` walks tiles tagged `street-1`, `street-2` and so on, in order, with pauses. Without it, calm troopers just wander, as NPCs do. | The flow field | Small to medium, nice to have | M1 |
| **The tracking fob** | A weapon slot with no attack, whose light blinks with the signal (a new weapon `onThink` hook), a beep whose speed follows the distance, a `setSignal` action, and a way to take the fob away. | Weapons | Small to medium | M1 |
| **Conversations** | NPC lines that move on each time you use them, lines that depend on story flags, an `on: 'talk'` trigger, and a `setFlag` action plus `if` conditions on triggers. Enough for the broker, the Jawa and the informant. Jan and Kyle's radio lines are plain messages (`JAN: ...`). | NPC lines, triggers | Small to medium | M1 |
| **Flying models** | A `fly` action moves a tagged model decoration along keyframes (position, height, facing, time), for the Crow landing and leaving and the shuttle leaving. It's only solid while it stands still. | Model decorations | Small | All |

| **Delays** | Actions that wait (`{ action: 'wait', seconds }`) inside a trigger's list, for drop-off timing and Jan's "two minutes". | Triggers | Small | All |
| **Moving characters** | `remove` and `place` actions, for Dace being taken, Mohc leaving and Dace reaching the pad. | Actions | Small | M1, M3 |

  { on: 'start', do: [
    { action: 'cutscene', camera: 'bay-rim', look: 'crow', seconds: 5 },

    { action: 'wait', seconds: 5 },
    { action: 'message', text: "JAN: Customs will be all over this bay in two minutes. I'm not staying for them." },
    { action: 'wait', seconds: 3 },
    { action: 'fly', tag: 'crow', path: [[20, 90, 0, 0], [20, 90, 12, 2], [60, 40, 40, 5]] },
    { action: 'spawn', thing: 'customs-officer', tag: 'bay-door' },
  ] },
  { on: 'talk', npc: 'jawa-trader', do: [{ action: 'setSignal', thing: 'dace' }] },
  { on: 'talk', npc: 'dace', do: ['hostile', { action: 'remove', thing: 'dace' }] },
  { on: 'hostile', do: [
    { action: 'closeDoors', tag: 'street-blast' },
    { action: 'spawn', thing: 'stormtrooper', tag: 'prefecture-door' },
    { action: 'music', id: 'combat' },
  ] },

```

**Engine limits to design around:** no driving (the family's speeder escape
happens off screen), no rooms over rooms, and no slopes (ramps are stairs).
Characters are drawn facing you from every angle, so a trooper on patrol
still faces you when seen from behind. That's the main visual cost of
Mission 1's calm half. The side and back views on the sandtrooper and Jawa
sheets would help (Phase 2's multi-angle stretch goal).

## Art and sound this needs

- **The Moldy Crow:** the most important new model, because it opens and
  closes every mission. Kyle's modified HWK-290, with glowing landing
  lights and engines, a few hundred faces, made in Blender and brought in
  with `tools/import-model.mjs`. Dark Forces' cutscenes are the reference.
  It also needs its engine roar (synthesized) and a dust effect for
  landings.
- **Sprites** (outsourced like the sandtrooper and Jawa sheets):
  - **The prototype:** big, crude and loud, with exposed cabling, a blade
    arm (a nod to Phase I) and a repeating blaster bolted on.
  - **Characters:** Dace (a scout trooper without his helmet), Mohc, the
    broker, a customs officer, a bartender, the informant, two to four
    kinds of townsfolk, Jawa traders, a Tusken Raider, a power droid and a
    dewback.
  - **Poses:** Mission 1's calm characters need idle and talk poses, not
    only walk and fire.
  - **Kyle and Jan** need no sprites: Kyle is the camera, and Jan stays in
    the cockpit. Portraits for the briefings would be nice later.
- **The fob:** a first-person sprite with its light lit and unlit, and the
  beep (synthesized).
- **Other 3D models:** vaporators, the derelict freighter, the cantina bar,
  stall awnings, barricades and domes.
- **Textures:** adobe walls, sand, wooden doors and awnings, and a day sky.
  The Dark Forces pack has no Tatooine set, so these are new.
- **Music:** a calm track for Mission 1's free roam, a cantina band track
  (original, not the film's), and the combat track the switch starts.
