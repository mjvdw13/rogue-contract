# Story and maps: first ideas

A draft to react to. Nothing here is decided yet. Two storylines, two new
maps that work with either one, and the engine work they would need.

The brief (Mark, 2026-10-13):

- Rooted in the original trilogy. It should feel like Dark Forces and *The
  Mandalorian*: close to the films, but not about their heroes.
- Plays to the engine's strengths. New engine features are fine if they stay
  realistic.
- It starts on Tatooine.
- Some parts aren't fights. You explore a place full of characters who would
  be enemies anywhere else, like stormtroopers on patrol who leave you alone.

## Common ground

Both ideas share these choices.

- **When:** about a year after *A New Hope*, between Yavin and Hoth, the same
  time as Dark Forces. The Empire is at full strength and angry. It knows the
  Death Star plans passed through Mos Eisley, so Tatooine is full of
  sandtroopers.
- **Who:** the mercenary from PLAN.md. You never see the hero, and the
  briefings talk to "you", so the hero's name and look can wait.
- **Where Mission 1 fits:** the Canyon Garrison we've built is a desert
  canyon at night with sandtroopers and Jawas. It's already a Tatooine
  mission, in the Jundland Wastes. Both ideas keep it as the main combat
  mission and put a calm Mos Eisley mission before it.
- **Pacing:** calm missions and combat missions take turns, and some
  missions switch from one to the other halfway through. The player learns
  that a stormtrooper is only dangerous when the situation turns.
- **Lore we lean on:** Mos Eisley and its cantina, docking bays, Jawas and
  sandcrawlers, Tusken Raiders, dewbacks, moisture farms, the Jundland
  Wastes, the Dune Sea, the Hutts, the Bounty Hunters' Guild and the
  Imperial Security Bureau (ISB). Every name in this file is a placeholder.

## Idea 1: Dead Drop (closest to Dark Forces)

**The setup.** An ISB major, Teodor Vass, runs a lockdown of Mos Eisley
called the Census. Every spacer is registered and every ship searched. Vass
has captured a Rebel cipher key, and he's using it to break the Alliance's
Outer Rim cells one by one. The Mos Eisley cell has gone quiet.

**The contract.** The Alliance can't send its own people, because the
Empire knows their faces. So it hires you: an outsider with a clean record.
Get into Mos Eisley, find the cell's dead drop, and stop the cipher before
Vass sends it to the fleet.

| # | Mission | Kind | What happens |
|---|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | Mos Eisley: Lockdown | Calm | Your contact, Pell Darro, was arrested this morning. You find his dead drop in a gutted astromech in a Jawa junk yard. It says where the cipher is (the canyon garrison) and how to get in (the vent). A forger in the back alleys sells you a travel pass, and you leave town in a hired speeder. |
| 2 | The Canyon Garrison | Combat | The map we have. You go in through the vent at night, steal the cipher from the data vault, overload the generator and get out by the landing pad. New: Darro is in a detention cell behind bars, and you free him. |
| 3 | The Homestead | Calm, then combat | The cell regroups at a moisture farm at dusk. At nightfall Vass's sandtroopers raid it: you were followed. |
| 4 | The Hutt's Palace (later) | Calm, then combat | Vass's aide sold a copy of the cipher to a Hutt, who is auctioning it. You walk in as a guest among Gamorreans, Weequay and other hunters. Then it goes loud. |

**What the calm parts feel like:** you're an outsider under suspicion. Each
checkpoint is a test, and your papers are fake.

**Mission 1's briefing, as a sample:**

> Mos Eisley is closed. Imperial customs board every ship that lands, and
> every spacer is logged. Our cell there missed its last two check-ins.
> Pell Darro will meet you in the cantina. Don't draw your weapon in town.
> The garrison has three hundred troopers, and you have one blaster.

## Idea 2: Bad Paper (closest to *The Mandalorian*)

**The setup.** You're a licensed bounty hunter with no side. The Guild's
broker in Mos Eisley has a quiet contract: Arlen Dace, a scout trooper who
deserted, is hiding somewhere in the Jundland. The pay is good, and alive
is preferred. The Empire puts up with hunters, so troopers wave you through.

**The twist.** The ISB posted the contract through a front. They don't want
Dace back. They want to know who's hiding him, and your tracking puck will
lead them there. Dace deserted because he refused an order to burn a farm,
and the garrison's data vault holds the orders for the rest of the
Jundland. When the raid comes, you're a loose end too. The contract has
gone rogue, which is where the title comes from.

| # | Mission | Kind | What happens |
|---|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | Mos Eisley: The Puck | Calm | You take the contract from the broker in a cantina booth. Troopers greet you as "hunter". You follow Dace's trail through people in town: a Jawa who sold him a speeder part, a farmer who bought supplies for "a cousin", a dewback handler who saw him ride out. |
| 2 | The Homestead | Calm, then combat | You find Dace at a moisture farm at dusk, and he tells you what he saw. At nightfall sandtroopers arrive, following your puck. The farmers escape and Dace is taken. A message says your hunting licence has been revoked. |
| 3 | The Canyon Garrison | Calm, then combat | The same map, now with a calm front half. The gate officer hasn't heard the news, so a licensed hunter walks in through the main gate. The snipers on the ledges watch you go. Troopers stand at their posts as you pass. Then the PA announces "Hunter's licence revoked. Detain on sight." and every trooper you walked past turns on you. You free Dace from detention, take the orders from the vault and get out by the pad. You can still use the vent, but the vent is off limits, so the alarm goes up sooner. |
| 4 | Off Tatooine (later) | | Dace's evidence goes to the Alliance, and the Alliance offers you work. From here it's the Dark Forces structure: a mercenary working for the Rebellion, as PLAN.md describes. |

**What the calm parts feel like:** you belong, until suddenly you don't.
The best moment is the garrison: the map and troopers you just walked
past, flipped by one announcement.

**Mission 1's briefing, as a sample:**

> One scout trooper, deserted, armed. Last seen in the Jundland. The
> client wants him breathing and pays double for it. The client didn't give
> a name. That's normal in this line of work.

## Which one

**My pick is Idea 2, possibly with Idea 1's Hutt palace added as a later
mission.** It earns the title. The hunting licence gives a reason inside
the story for troopers to leave you alone, so calm parts can happen in
Imperial bases as well as in town. It also gets more out of the Canyon
Garrison: walking calmly through a place you'll fight your way out of is
the strongest version of "enemies who aren't fighting you". And it still
ends where PLAN.md starts, with a mercenary working for the Rebellion.

Idea 1 is the safer one. It's closer to Dark Forces and keeps the plan's
premise from the first mission, but its calm parts happen only in towns
and palaces.

## Map A: Mos Eisley (Mission 1 in both ideas)

A calm hub in daylight. Most characters can be talked to, and nobody
shoots unless you give them a reason.

- **Size:** about 150 × 110 tiles (75 × 55 m). Mission 1 is 128 × 100.
- **Light:** sunlit streets at 200-224 under a new day sky with both suns,
  alleys and doorways in shade at 136-152, and dim interiors. The garrison
  is at night, so this is the first daylight map.
- **Buildings:** in Doom's style. The street's sky sits just above the
  rooftops, so a building's wall stops at its roofline and the sky shows
  above it. Rooftops you can walk on are raised floors under a higher sky.
  Round adobe shapes come from chamfers and octagons (angled walls), and
  domes are 3D models.

```
 west                                     ║                       east
  [4 Jawa junk yard]  [7 alleys, forger]  ║  [8 prefecture, cells]
          |                 |             ║          |
  ======= 2 main street (open) =======  [5 checkpoint] == street (Imperial) == [9 east gate]
          |                 |             ║
  [1 Docking Bay 87]   [3 cantina]        ║  [6 rooftops: a way over 5]
```

| # | Area | Notes |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Docking Bay 87 (start) | A round bay sunk 3 below the street, open to the sky, with your ship in the middle (a 3D model). Stairs go up to a 4 × 4 bay door. Inside are a power droid and the bay owner ("Docking fees doubled. Imperials."). A customs officer and two troopers stand at the bay door and check your papers or licence. They teach the first rule: holster your weapon. |
| 2 | Main street | Wide (10-14 tiles), sand, adobe walls. Market stalls with awnings (3D models) and crates. A sandtrooper patrol with a dewback walks up and down. Jawas argue over a droid. A comm tower above the roofs is the landmark. |
| 3 | The cantina | A dark entrance, then steps down into a round main room (an octagon about 24 across). A round bar stands in the middle (a 3D model or an angled-wall island). Booths sit round the edge under lower ceilings. The band has its own music, which starts when you walk in (the existing `music` action). There's a bartender, other customers, and two troopers whose patrol passes through. Your contact's (or the broker's) booth is here. |
| 4 | Jawa junk yard | A yard of droid parts behind a cage fence, with Jawa traders. Here they're calm characters, a different definition from the canyon's hostile Jawas. Idea 1: the dead drop is in a gutted astromech (use it). Idea 2: a Jawa remembers the part Dace bought. |
| 5 | Imperial checkpoint | Barricades across the street (low railings and 3D barrier models), two troopers and an officer. Past it is a restricted zone. Without a pass you're turned back once. Push past and the alarm goes up. |
| 6 | Rooftops | Crates and a collapsed wall let you climb onto flat roofs. You cross them with running jumps (up to 4 tiles) and drop into a yard behind the checkpoint. A trooper posted on one roof can see part of the route, so you time your crossing. |
| 7 | Back alleys | Narrow, angled and dark (light 120). The forger (Idea 1) or an informant (Idea 2) lives here. They want a debt collected from a thug in a back room. Closed doors already stop sound in the engine, so that fight doesn't alert the street. |
| 8 | Prefecture (restricted) | Holding cells behind bars, seen from the alley through a barred window. Idea 1: Darro is in one, out of reach for now. Idea 2: a terminal shows Dace's wanted file. |
| 9 | East gate | The exit checkpoint and a speeder lot. Show your pass (or go round over the roofs), then use a hired landspeeder (a 3D model) to end the level. |

**If you raise the alarm:** blast doors close across the main street, the
troopers who hear it turn hostile, and a squad comes out of the prefecture.
The mission doesn't fail. The Jawas' back gate out of the junk yard becomes
the way out: harder, but you can still finish.

**Secrets:** a cantina back room (power cells and a piece of lore), a
maintenance pit under your ship (a shield unit), and a rooftop cache with a
better weapon.

## Map B: The Homestead (Mission 3 in Idea 1, Mission 2 in Idea 2)

A moisture farm at the edge of the Jundland. It's calm at dusk, then raided
at nightfall. This map shows the switch from calm to combat in the middle
of a level.

- **Size:** about 110 × 90 tiles.
- **Light:** dusk at 168 under a sunset sky. When the raid starts, the sky
  changes to night, the outdoors drops to 128-144, and the courtyard is lit
  only from its doorways.

```
            [7 ridge: Tusken lookout]
                     |
  [1 arrival, speeder] -> [2 vaporator field] -> [3 entry dome]
                               |                       | stairs down
                     open rim: look down               v
                          [4 sunken courtyard, -6, sky]
                      doors to [5 rooms] west, south, east
                      [6 garage] -> long stairs up -> south surface
```

| # | Area | Notes |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Arrival | Your speeder parked on a ridge path that drops down to the farm. |
| 2 | Vaporator field | Rows of moisture vaporators (tall 3D models), low dunes (raised floors), a farmhand and a wandering power droid. |
| 3 | Entry dome | A small round building over an enclosed stairwell down to the courtyard. |
| 4 | Sunken courtyard | A round pit open to the sky, about 20 across, 6 below the ground. From the open north rim you look down into it, which the height grid does well. |
| 5 | Rooms | Off the courtyard: a common room, storage, a hideout or a comms room (who you came to find), and a cellar with a dark cistern. |
| 6 | Garage | A speeder (3D model) and a long stair up to the surface. The farmers escape this way. |
| 7 | Ridge | Rocks above the farm where Tuskens watch. They leave you alone in the calm half. |

**The switch:** once you've talked to the person you came for (Dace, or the
cell), a trigger changes the light and sky, plays an engine roar and the
combat music, and spawns sandtroopers on the ridge path and at the garage
stair. Civilians run for the garage. In the fight that follows, troopers on
the rim shoot down into the pit, and you retake the rim by the stairwell or
the garage stair. If Tuskens and troopers can fight each other (see
factions below), the ridge becomes a three-way fight.

**A grid check first:** the engine can't put rooms under ground you can
walk on. So the rooms round the courtyard sit behind its walls with nothing
walkable over them, and from the surface the far side of the pit looks like
the homestead's raised outer wall. Try the courtyard in the sandbox before
building the map.

## The Canyon Garrison, per idea

- **Idea 1:** as `docs/mission1.md` describes, plus a short detention block
  off the barracks corridor: bars (existing fences) and a cell door you
  open.
- **Idea 2:** the same, plus a calm front half. The main gate opens for you
  after you talk to the gate officer. The snipers, the gate guards and the
  barracks troopers start calm, and the Jawas pick through a scrap heap
  instead of shooting. A trigger at the red door, or a timer, plays the PA
  announcement and raises the alarm for the whole level. The vent is
  restricted, so going in that way raises the alarm early.

## Engine work these need

Most of this builds on code we already have: the friendly NPCs
(`things/npc.js`: wandering, lines when bumped), monster wake-up
(`things/monster.js`), the noise flood fill (`world/noise.js`), and
triggers and actions.

| Feature | What it does | Built from | Size | Needed by |
|---------|--------------|------------|------|-----------|
| **Calm enemies and the alarm** | A placed monster can start `calm`. It patrols or stands its post and says lines when you bump or use it, the way NPCs do, and doesn't attack. The alarm makes it a normal monster. The alarm goes up if you fire near calm enemies (the noise fill), hit anyone, are seen in a restricted tagged area, ignore three warnings, or a trigger calls it. Its reach is either earshot or the whole level. | NPC and monster code, noise | Medium | Both |
| **Patrol routes** | `patrol: 'street'` walks tiles tagged `street-1`, `street-2` and so on, in order, with pauses. | The flow field | Small to medium | Both |
| **Holstering** | A no-weapon slot. If a calm trooper sees you with a weapon drawn, you get a warning line, and enough warnings raise the alarm. | Weapons, calm enemies | Small | Both |
| **Conversations** | NPC lines that move on each time you use them, lines that depend on story flags, an `on: 'talk'` trigger, and a `setFlag` action plus `if` conditions on triggers. | NPC lines, triggers | Small to medium | Both |
| **Objectives and briefings** | Already planned in Phase 2. | | Medium | Both |
| **Sky and time of day** | A day sky with both suns, and a `setSky` action for dusk to night. | Sky, `setLight` | Small | Both |
| **Factions** | Tuskens and troopers shoot each other on sight. | Monster infighting (`m.target`) | Small | Optional |
| **Running away** | Civilians run from gunfire to a tagged spot or away from the shooter. | NPC movement | Small | Nice to have |
| **Removing a character** | A `remove` action, for scripted moments like Dace being taken. | Actions | Small | Idea 2 |
| **Transmissions** | A portrait and a text box for a handler's messages during a mission. | HUD | Small | Optional |
| **Tinted fog** | Distance fades to sand colour instead of black, for a dust storm in the Dune Sea. | Colormaps | Medium | Later |

A placed calm trooper could look like this:

```js
{ type: 'stormtrooper', x: 40.5, y: 22.5, calm: { patrol: 'street', lines: ['Move along.', 'Papers.'] } }
// on the level:
alarm: { warnings: 3, reach: 'earshot', restricted: ['prefecture', 'roof-watch'] },
triggers: [{ on: 'alarm', do: [{ action: 'closeDoors', tag: 'street-blast' }] }],
```

**Engine limits to design around:** no driving (speeder trips happen
between missions), no rooms over rooms, and no slopes (ramps are stairs).
Characters are drawn facing you from every angle, so a trooper seen from
behind on patrol still faces you. That's the biggest visual cost of calm
missions, because you see patrols from the side and back far more often
than enemies charging at you. The side and back views on the sandtrooper
and Jawa sheets help (Phase 2's multi-angle stretch goal).

## Art and sound these need

- **Sprites** (outsourced like the sandtrooper and Jawa sheets): a Tusken
  Raider, two to four kinds of townsfolk (spacer, moisture farmer, Rodian,
  Aqualish), a power droid, a dewback, and a bartender. Calm characters need
  idle and talk poses, not only walk and fire.
- **3D models:** vaporators, a landspeeder, your ship (in place of the
  shuttle placeholder), the cantina bar, stall awnings, barricades, domes.
- **Textures:** adobe walls, sand, wooden doors and awnings, and a day sky
  and a dusk sky. The Dark Forces pack has no Tatooine set, so these are
  new.
- **Music:** a calm exploration track, a cantina band track (original, not
  the film's), and a combat track for when the alarm goes up. This links to
  Phase 2's iMUSE-style switching.

## Open questions

2. Mos Eisley before the Canyon Garrison (my suggestion), or keep the
   garrison as the first mission so the slice opens with combat?
3. How strict should calm missions be? My suggestion: warnings first, an

   way out on every calm map.
4. About a year after *A New Hope*, or after *Return of the Jedi*, closer to
   *The Mandalorian* (Imperial remnants, a weaker Empire)? I'd stay in the
   original trilogy's time: it has more sandtroopers and a stronger Empire
   to hide from.
5. The hero's name, and whether they say anything in messages.
6. How many Tatooine missions before leaving the planet: three, or four
   with the Hutt palace?
