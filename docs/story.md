# Story: Bad Paper, a Dark Forces prequel

The story for Rogue Contract. The direction is chosen (below), and the
details are still a draft to refine.

## Decided so far (Mark, 2026-10-13)

- **Bad Paper**, the bounty-hunter idea (Idea 2 of the first draft). The
  other idea, Dead Drop, is set aside, though its Hutt palace could come
  back as a later mission.
- **A prequel to Dark Forces.** It's set a few months before Dark Forces'
  first mission (Danuta, where Kyle Katarn steals the Death Star plans), so
  shortly before *A New Hope*. Dark Forces belongs to the old "Legends"
  continuity, and *Rogue One* tells the plans' theft differently. Where the
  two disagree, we follow Dark Forces.
- **The tracking fob is a mechanic:** see below.

Assumed until you say otherwise:

- Mos Eisley comes before the Canyon Garrison, so the game opens calm.
- An alarm makes a calm mission harder but never fails it, and every calm
  map has a loud way out.

The brief, for reference: rooted in the original trilogy and close to Dark
Forces and *The Mandalorian*; playing to the engine's strengths; starting on
Tatooine; with parts that aren't fights, where characters who are enemies
elsewhere leave you alone.

## How it fits Dark Forces

In Dark Forces, General Rom Mohc builds the Dark Troopers: droid soldiers in
three phases, armoured in phrik, a metal mined on Gromas 16 that blasters
can't get through. The Rebellion learns they exist only after one wipes out
its base on Talay (Dark Forces' second mission).

Our link: **the Canyon Garrison on Tatooine is an early field test of
Mohc's project.** It's a prototype from before Phase I, a crude assault
droid built before Mohc had phrik, so blasters do hurt it. This is Phase
3's planned mini-boss ("a prototype assault droid").

- **Why Tatooine:** it's far from the Core, nobody asks questions, and the
  Jundland Wastes are full of targets: Tusken camps and isolated farms.
- **What the hero does:** destroys the prototype and gets the test records
  out. The test failed, and in Legends terms that's why Mohc needs a tougher
  armour, and so phrik.
- **The irony of a prequel:** the records reach the Rebellion through Jan
  Ors (Kyle Katarn's partner and pilot in Dark Forces). They're filed as
  unconfirmed: one prototype, destroyed, no sign of others. Months later,
  nobody on Talay has read them.
- **Dark Forces characters:** Jan Ors at the end, and a glimpse of Mohc. He
  never fights you, because he's Dark Forces' final boss. Kyle Katarn stays
  out, as PLAN.md decided ("no Kyle Katarn retelling").

## The story

**You** are a licensed bounty hunter with no side. You never see the hero,
and the briefings talk to "you".

**The contract.** The Guild's broker in Mos Eisley has a quiet job: Arlen
Dace, a scout trooper who deserted, is hiding somewhere in the Jundland. The
pay is good, and alive is preferred. The Empire puts up with licensed
hunters, so troopers wave you through.

**What Dace knows.** His scout unit guarded the garrison's test range. He
watched the prototype destroy a Tusken camp. Then his unit was ordered to
clear a moisture farm so the prototype could be tested on it. He refused,
warned the family and ran. They've hidden him ever since.

**The twist.** Mohc's people posted the contract through a front. They don't
want Dace back. They want him silenced, and they want to know who he told.
Your fob carries an Imperial tracer, and it leads them to the farm. Once
the raid comes, you're a loose end too. The contract has gone rogue.

## The tracking fob

Taken from the tracking fob in *The Mandalorian*: a small handheld unit whose
light blinks faster as you close in on your bounty. Here it's a tool, then a
betrayal, then a weapon.

**How it works:**

- **You hold it like a weapon.** It has its own slot, and its first-person
  sprite shows its blinking light. While you hold it, your blaster is
  holstered, so in calm missions it's the natural thing to carry, and it
  never makes troopers suspicious.
- **It tracks one signal at a time.** It blinks and beeps faster as you get
  closer. Its light turns from amber to green when you face the signal, and
  it stays dark when there's nothing to track. Triggers set the signal: a
  tagged spot, an item or a character.
- **Distance shows.** Far away, a weak signal blinks slowly: enough to show
  the way, not enough to find a door.

**How it changes over the story:**

| Mission | The fob is | What it adds |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| Mos Eisley | A hunting tool | It follows leads: dark at first, then a faint signal east from the Jawas' code, then a fix from the comm tower. |
| The Homestead | The betrayal | It leads you to Dace, and the Imperials follow it to you both. After Dace finds the tracer, you know every trooper who comes is riding your signal. The tracer can't come out without breaking the fob. |
| The Canyon Garrison | A weapon that cuts both ways | It picks up Dace's prisoner tag and leads you to his cell. After the alarm, though, the garrison tracks the fob: while you carry it, the hunting troopers close in on you. Fire with the fob to throw it. It keeps transmitting where it lands, and the troopers search there instead. Pick it up again to keep tracking. In the final fight you throw it beside the reactor to pull the prototype in, then overload the generator. The fob is destroyed with it, and so is the last of your old job. |

## Map A: Mos Eisley (Mission 1)

A calm hub in daylight. Most characters can be talked to, and nobody
shoots unless you give them a reason.

- **Size:** about 150 × 110 tiles (75 × 55 m). The Canyon Garrison is
  128 × 100.
- **Light:** sunlit streets at 200-224 under a new day sky with both suns,
  alleys and doorways in shade at 136-152, and dim interiors. The garrison
  is at night, so this is the first daylight map.
- **Buildings:** in Doom's style. The street's sky sits just above the
  rooftops, so a building's wall stops at its roofline and the sky shows
  above it. Rooftops you can walk on are raised floors under a higher sky.
  Round adobe shapes come from chamfers and octagons (angled walls), and
  domes are 3D models.

```
 west                                     ║                        east
  [4 Jawa junk yard]  [7 alleys]          ║  [8 prefecture]  [10 comm tower]
          |                 |             ║          |              |
  ======= 2 main street (open) =======  [5 checkpoint] == street (Imperial) == [9 east gate]
          |                 |             ║
  [1 Docking Bay 87]   [3 cantina]        ║  [6 rooftops: a way over 5]
```

**Route:** the bay → the cantina (contract and fob) → the junk yard (a
faint signal) → past the checkpoint, with a pass or over the roofs → the
comm tower (a fix) → the east gate.

| # | Area | Notes |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Docking Bay 87 (start) | A round bay sunk 3 below the street, open to the sky, with your ship in the middle (a 3D model). Stairs go up to a 4 × 4 bay door. Inside are a power droid and the bay owner ("Docking fees doubled. Imperials."). A customs officer and two troopers stand at the bay door and check your licence. They teach the first rule: keep your blaster holstered. |
| 2 | Main street | Wide (10-14 tiles), sand, adobe walls. Market stalls with awnings (3D models) and crates. A sandtrooper patrol with a dewback walks up and down. Jawas argue over a droid. The probe droid hovers over the market. The comm tower above the roofs is the landmark. |
| 3 | The cantina | A dark entrance, then steps down into a round main room (an octagon about 24 across). A round bar stands in the middle (a 3D model or an angled-wall island). Booths sit round the edge under lower ceilings. The band has its own music, which starts when you walk in (the existing `music` action). There's a bartender, other customers, and two troopers whose patrol passes through. The broker's booth is at the back: the contract and the fob. |
| 4 | Jawa junk yard | A yard of droid parts behind a cage fence, with Jawa traders. Here they're calm characters, a different definition from the canyon's Jawas. A trader remembers selling Dace a power coupling, and for a price gives you its transponder code. |
| 5 | Imperial checkpoint | Barricades across the street (low railings and 3D barrier models), two troopers and an officer. "Your licence is Guild business. The relay is Imperial business." Past it is a restricted zone. Without a pass you're turned back once. Push past and the alarm goes up. |
| 6 | Rooftops | Crates and a collapsed wall let you climb onto flat roofs. You cross them with running jumps (up to 4 tiles) and drop into a yard behind the checkpoint. A trooper posted on one roof can see part of the route, so you time your crossing. |
| 7 | Back alleys | Narrow, angled and dark (light 120). An informant sells technicians' passes, if you collect a debt from a thug in a back room first. Closed doors already stop sound in the engine, so that fight doesn't alert the street. |
| 8 | Prefecture (restricted) | Holding cells behind bars, and a terminal with Dace's wanted file. It lists his unit, "Jundland Range security", the first hint of the garrison. |
| 9 | East gate | The exit checkpoint and a speeder lot. Use a hired landspeeder (a 3D model) to end the level. |
| 10 | Comm tower (restricted) | A lift inside the tower rises to a relay platform with a view over the whole town. Using the relay turns the faint signal into a fix on the farm, and the fob points to the east gate. |

**If you raise the alarm:** blast doors close across the main street, the
troopers who hear it turn hostile, and a squad comes out of the prefecture.
The Jawas' back gate out of the junk yard becomes the way out: harder, but
you can still finish.

**Secrets:** a cantina back room (power cells and a piece of lore), a
maintenance pit under your ship (a shield unit), and a rooftop cache with a
better weapon.

## Map B: The Homestead (Mission 2)

The farm Dace saved, at the edge of the Jundland. It's calm at dusk, then
raided at nightfall. This map shows the switch from calm to combat in the
middle of a level, and the first sight of the prototype.

- **Size:** about 110 × 90 tiles.
- **Light:** dusk at 168 under a sunset sky. When the raid starts, the sky
  changes to night, the outdoors drops to 128-144, and the courtyard is lit
  only from its doorways.

```
            [7 ridge: Tusken lookout]
                     |
  [1 arrival, speeder] -> [2 vaporator field] -> [3 entry dome]
                               |                       | stairs down
                     open rim: look down               v
                          [4 sunken courtyard, -6, sky]
                      doors to [5 rooms] west, south, east
                      [6 garage] -> long stairs up -> south surface
```

| # | Area | Notes |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Arrival | Your speeder parked on a ridge path that drops down to the farm. |
| 2 | Vaporator field | Rows of moisture vaporators (tall 3D models), low dunes (raised floors), a farmhand and a wandering power droid. In the raid, the prototype walks through here. |
| 3 | Entry dome | A small round building over an enclosed stairwell down to the courtyard. |
| 4 | Sunken courtyard | A round pit open to the sky, about 20 across, 6 below the ground. From the open north rim you look down into it, which the height grid does well. |
| 5 | Rooms | Off the courtyard: a common room, storage, and a cellar with a dark cistern where Dace hides. The family won't say where he is, but the fob does. |
| 6 | Garage | A speeder (3D model) and a long stair up to the surface. The family escapes this way, and so do you. |
| 7 | Ridge | Rocks above the farm where Tuskens watch. They leave you alone in the calm half. |

**The switch:** once Dace finds the tracer, a trigger changes the light and
sky, plays an engine roar and the combat music, and spawns sandtroopers on
the ridge path and at the garage stair. The family runs for the garage.
Troopers on the rim shoot down into the pit, and you retake the rim by the
stairwell or the garage stair. When the troopers are thinned out, the
prototype walks in from the vaporator field: heavy footsteps, then its
shape in the dark. It's far too tough to fight here, and the objective says
run. Dace is taken in the confusion. If Tuskens and troopers can fight each
other (see factions below), the ridge becomes a three-way fight.

**A grid check first:** the engine can't put rooms under ground you can
walk on. So the rooms round the courtyard sit behind its walls with nothing
walkable over them, and from the surface the far side of the pit looks like
the homestead's raised outer wall. Try the courtyard in the sandbox before
building the map.

## Map C: The Canyon Garrison (Mission 3, changes to the existing map)

`docs/mission1.md` describes the map. This story changes it like this:

- **A calm front half.** The main gate opens after you talk to the gate
  officer. The snipers, the gate guards and the barracks troopers start
  calm, and the Jawas pick through a scrap heap instead of shooting. The
  vent is off limits, so going in that way raises the alarm early.
- **Mohc behind the barred window.** The existing window from the barracks
  corridor into the officer's office shows Mohc and the garrison's commander
  (characters you can't shoot). Walking up to it plays their conversation as
  messages, sets the fob's signal to Dace's cell, and starts the PA
  announcement and a level-wide alarm. Mohc leaves before you can reach him.
- **A detention block** off the barracks corridor, behind bars (existing
  fences), where the fob leads you. You open Dace's cell, and he heads for
  the pad on his own. He's removed here and placed at the pad, so he needs
  no escort AI.
- **The vault** holds the test records, the mission's objective.
- **The prototype in the generator chamber,** as the boss. Blasters wear it
  down slowly. Throw the fob by the reactor to pull it in, then overload
  the generator, which finishes it.
- **The pad:** Mohc's shuttle is gone. Jan Ors's ship (a 3D model) lands at
  the pickup spot, which is already the exit.

## Engine work this needs

Most of this builds on code we already have: the friendly NPCs
(`things/npc.js`: wandering, lines when bumped), monster wake-up
(`things/monster.js`), the noise flood fill (`world/noise.js`), custom
attacks (`defineAttack`), and triggers and actions.

| Feature | What it does | Built from | Size |
|---------|--------------|------------|------|
| **Calm enemies and the alarm** | A placed monster can start `calm`. It patrols or stands its post and says lines when you bump or use it, the way NPCs do, and doesn't attack. The alarm makes it a normal monster. The alarm goes up if you fire near calm enemies (the noise fill), hit anyone, are seen in a restricted tagged area, ignore three warnings, or a trigger calls it. Its reach is either earshot or the whole level. | NPC and monster code, noise | Medium |
| **The tracking fob** | A weapon slot with no attack, whose light blinks with the signal (a new weapon `onThink` hook). A beep whose speed follows the distance. A `setSignal` action. Throwing it is a custom attack that spawns a fob you can pick up again. While a level's `traced` flag is on, hunting monsters head for wherever the fob is. | Weapons, `defineAttack`, items | Medium |
| **Searching** | A monster walks to a point, looks around, then gives up or attacks if it sees you. The fob needs it, and so do calm troopers who hear something. | Monster AI, flow field | Small to medium |
| **Patrol routes** | `patrol: 'street'` walks tiles tagged `street-1`, `street-2` and so on, in order, with pauses. | The flow field | Small to medium |
| **Holstering** | If a calm trooper sees you with a weapon drawn, you get a warning line, and enough warnings raise the alarm. Holding the fob counts as holstered. | Calm enemies | Small |
| **Conversations** | NPC lines that move on each time you use them, lines that depend on story flags, an `on: 'talk'` trigger, and a `setFlag` action plus `if` conditions on triggers. | NPC lines, triggers | Small to medium |
| **Objectives and briefings** | Already planned in Phase 2. | | Medium |
| **Sky and time of day** | A day sky with both suns, a sunset sky, and a `setSky` action for dusk to night. | Sky, `setLight` | Small |
| **Moving characters** | `remove` and `place` actions, for Dace being taken, Mohc leaving and Dace reaching the pad. | Actions | Small |
| **Damage from a trigger** | A `damage { tag, amount }` action, so the overload hurts the prototype. | Actions | Small |
| **Factions** | Tuskens and troopers shoot each other on sight. | Monster infighting (`m.target`) | Small, optional |
| **Running away** | Civilians run from gunfire to a tagged spot or away from the shooter. | NPC movement | Small, nice to have |

A placed calm trooper could look like this:

```js
{ type: 'stormtrooper', x: 40.5, y: 22.5, calm: { patrol: 'street', lines: ['Move along.', 'Hunter.'] } }
// on the level:
alarm: { warnings: 3, reach: 'earshot', restricted: ['prefecture', 'comm-tower', 'roof-watch'] },
triggers: [
  { on: 'alarm', do: [{ action: 'closeDoors', tag: 'street-blast' }] },
  { on: 'enter', tag: 'relay', do: [{ action: 'setSignal', tag: 'east-gate' }] },
],
```

**Engine limits to design around:** no driving (speeder trips happen
between missions), no rooms over rooms, and no slopes (ramps are stairs).
Characters are drawn facing you from every angle, so a trooper on patrol
still faces you when seen from behind. That's the biggest visual cost of
calm missions, because you see patrols from the side and back far more
often than enemies charging at you. The side and back views on the
sandtrooper and Jawa sheets help (Phase 2's multi-angle stretch goal).

## Art and sound this needs

- **Sprites** (outsourced like the sandtrooper and Jawa sheets):
  - **The prototype:** big, crude and loud, with exposed cabling, a blade
    arm (a nod to Phase I) and a repeating blaster bolted on.
  - **Characters:** Dace (a scout trooper without his helmet), Mohc and the
    garrison's commander (officers), the broker, the farm family, a
    bartender, two to four kinds of townsfolk, a Tusken Raider, a power
    droid and a dewback.
  - **Poses:** calm characters need idle and talk poses, not only walk and
    fire.
- **The fob:** a first-person sprite with its light lit and unlit, and the
  beep (synthesized).
- **3D models:** vaporators, a landspeeder, your ship, Jan Ors's ship, the
  cantina bar, stall awnings, barricades, domes, and the comm tower's top.
- **Textures:** adobe walls, sand, wooden doors and awnings, and a day sky
  and a sunset sky. The Dark Forces pack has no Tatooine set, so these are
  new.
- **Music:** a calm exploration track, a cantina band track (original, not
  the film's), and a combat track for when the alarm goes up. This links to
  Phase 2's iMUSE-style switching.
